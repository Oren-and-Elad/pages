Section 6 · Visual privacy and overlooking SEE · p. 17–19

GSA 2021-087 · SEE · page 17
6.1 The relationship to No. 10 and No. 6

The balcony sits at first-floor level on the rear elevation. The rear private open space of No. 10 lies about 9 m to the south-east, and the living room windows of No. 6 about 11 m to the north-east. Without treatment, a person standing at the balustrade would have a direct, elevated view into both.

6.2 Design response

The balcony depth has been reduced from 2.4 m to 1.8 m, which keeps it ancillary to the main bedroom. It is not the primary private open space; that remains the ground-floor terrace and lawn. Fixed privacy screens 1.6 m above the finished floor level are proposed to the eastern and western edges. The first-floor windows are offset from those of the adjoining dwellings, and screen planting of a mature height of 3–4 m is proposed along the southern boundary (landscape plan L-01).

6.3 Assessment

The sightline sections at Appendix C show that, with the screens in place, there is no direct sightline from the balcony into the rear private open space or living-room windows of either adjoining dwelling. The proposal meets the visual privacy objectives of Randwick DCP 2013 Part C1 and is consistent with the techniques the DCP itself lists: locating windows to limit overlooking, offsetting windows, screening, and planting.
