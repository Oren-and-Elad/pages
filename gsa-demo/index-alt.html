4.6 Visual privacy

Randwick Comprehensive DCP 2013 — Part C1 Low Density Residential, visual privacy objectives and controls

The proposed first-floor balcony is located at the rear of the dwelling and is accessed solely from the main bedroom. It has a depth of 1.5 m and an area of 6.2 m². At this depth the balcony functions as an ancillary outdoor space to a bedroom rather than as a primary living or entertaining area, and the intensity of its use is correspondingly low. This is consistent with the DCP objective of minimising overlooking of the private open space and habitable rooms of adjoining dwellings.

Source: matter 2021-047, SEE p.6

A fixed privacy screen is proposed along the full length of the northern (side) edge of the balcony, extending to a height of 1.6 m above the finished floor level. The screen comprises fixed, angled aluminium louvres with a maximum open area of 25%, oriented to direct views downward and towards the subject site's own rear yard. The screen prevents direct sightlines from the balcony to the rear private open space of No. 25 and to the living-room windows on its southern elevation.

Source: matter 2021-047, SEE p.6

Sightlines from the balcony towards the rear boundary are offset from the neighbouring private open space by the balcony's location against the southern side of the rear elevation and by a 0.6 m deep planter box along the rear edge, which draws the standing line back from the balustrade. Sightline diagrams at Appendix C (Drawings DA-21 and DA-22) demonstrate that views at a standing eye height of 1.6 m are directed to the subject site's own rear garden and to the rear lane.

Source: matter 2021-047, SEE p.7

Having regard to the reduced depth, the fixed screening to 1.6 m, the offset sightlines and the planter boxes, the proposal satisfies the visual privacy objectives of Part C1 of the Randwick Comprehensive DCP 2013. The amenity of the adjoining private open space and habitable rooms is maintained, and no additional conditions of consent are considered necessary beyond those securing the screen as a permanent, fixed element.

Source: matter 2021-047, SEE p.7
